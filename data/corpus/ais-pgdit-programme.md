# Postgraduate Diploma in Information Technology (PGDIT) Programme

## Programme details

The Postgraduate Diploma in Information Technology (PGDIT) at Auckland
Institute of Studies (AIS) is a Level 8 qualification of 120 credits, with a
typical duration of 12 months full-time. The PGDIT has been designed to fill
the global skills shortage recognised in the IT sector. Graduates will be
highly employable and have the opportunity to enter the IT industry at a
higher-than entry-level position. The programme's flexible pathways also cater
for students wanting to pursue a career in research.

## Programme structure

The PGDIT comprises 5 courses corresponding to one of three specialist topics:
Software Development, Information Systems, or Networks and Security, as well
as an Internship, Research, or Industry Project pathway. The PGDIT is
prescriptive and the order in which courses are taken varies by intake;
enrolment in courses is subject to academic approval.

## Specialist topics

The Software Development specialist topic enables students to analyse
requirements and design, implement, test and deploy a software application.
Its courses include SOFT806 Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment,
SOFT807 Cloud Application Development, and SOFT808 Software User Experience.
The Information Systems specialist topic covers the acquisition, storage,
transformation and analysis of data and information within a business setting.
Its courses include INFO812 Data Mining, INFO813 Artificial Intelligence, and
INFO814 Enterprise Cloud-based Systems. The Networks and Security specialist
topic deals with the technical issues involved in designing, implementing,
administering and securing computer networks. Its courses include CONE809
Topics in Cloud Computing, CONE810 Computer and Communication Network
Security, and CONE811 Penetration Testing. Some courses can be replaced with
COMP805 Specialisation Project or COMP722 Information Technology Industry
Practice with approval from the Academic Head of Programme.

## Pathways

Each specialist topic can be completed via one of three pathways. The COMP802
Research Project pathway requires students to also take COMP801 Research
Methods in their first trimester of study. The COMP803 Internship pathway
consists of 15 hours a week for 26 weeks, totalling 390 hours. The COMP804
Industrial Project pathway consists of a two-trimester supervised project from
local industry. COMP806 Intensive Internship is completed over one trimester.

## Entry requirements

Entry to the PGDIT requires a Bachelor's degree or equivalent in Information
Technology, Computer Science or a related field of study, or a Graduate
Diploma or equivalent in Information Technology, Computer Science or a related
field of study. Applicants also require an English level of IELTS 6.5 with no
individual band lower than 6.0 / TOEFL iBT 79 (with a writing score of 21) /
PToE academic score of 58 with no band lower than 50 / NZCEL (Academic)
Level 5.

## Fees

For international students, the 2025 programme fees are NZ$24,760 plus
resource fees of NZ$2,280, a total of NZ$27,040. For domestic students, the
2025 programme fees are NZ$6,136 plus resource fees of NZ$2,280, a total of
NZ$8,416. Both are subject to the compulsory Student Services Fee of NZ$300
per year (or NZ$30 per month or part thereof).

## Start dates

The PGDIT is 12 months full-time with three intakes per year (2025 start
dates: 3 February, 26 May and 15 September).
